Research Interests

I focus on two connected questions: when suicide risk rises within a person, and how mental health services can respond in time.

Research Goals

To help reduce suicide rates, I aim to pursue two complementary research goals.

Individual Level

Risk dynamics · EMA · machine learning

How can EMA, intensive longitudinal data, and supervised machine learning help predict when suicide risk will rise within an individual?

System Level

Hotlines · implementation · cost-effectiveness

How can suicide prevention hotlines and other crisis-response services provide cost-effective support at scale?

How can digital interventions deliver timely and accessible support to people at risk?

What Shaped My Research Interests

Experiences and evidence behind my research goals

My interest in suicide prevention grew from living in South Korea, where suicide rates remain high, working within an overstretched mental health care system, and studying how risk unfolds over time.

03 · What a single assessment can miss

Suicidal thoughts can change within hours—and risk pathways differ across people

1–3 h Average duration of elevated suicidal thinking in a real-time study Coppersmith et al., PNAS · 2023
35–41% Of adolescents reporting suicidal thoughts during EMA later denied them retrospectively Shin et al., JCPP Advances · 2025
51,424 Repeated observations across two samples revealed distinct person-specific risk pathways Coppersmith et al., Behav. Res. Ther. · 2024

During my clinical work, I have heard someone say they wanted to die, then report feeling better the next day. Seeing this pattern repeatedly made me question what single assessments might miss. High-frequency studies show that suicidal thoughts can change within hours, and that risk pathways differ across individuals.

Researching addiction, genetic vulnerability, and longitudinal change—including my own intensive daily-diary study—has taught me to look beyond group averages. I want to build on that foundation to identify when suicide risk rises within a particular person, rather than relying only on group-level risk estimates. I see this as a promising direction for prevention in Korea and beyond.

Research sources: Coppersmith et al. (2023) · Shin, Gratch & Cha (2025) · Coppersmith et al. (2024) · Wang et al. (2024).
04 · Why I studied addiction

Understanding Suicide Through the Lens of Addiction

Suicide prevention has been my central interest since I first learned about South Korea’s high suicide rate. I turned to addiction research to better understand self-destructive behavior and its relationship with suicide. In clinical practice, meeting people who struggled with both addiction and suicidal thoughts reinforced that connection.

40.3% of suicide attempters were intoxicated at the time of the attempt in a Korean two-hospital study (n = 347). Jin et al. · Mood and Emotion (2013)
+94% higher risk of death by suicide associated with alcohol use in a meta-analysis of 33 longitudinal studies. Isaacs et al. · Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior (2022)

My work on addiction, genetic vulnerability, and longitudinal change has given me a foundation to pursue suicide prevention more directly.

Research sources: Jin et al. (2013; Korea) · Isaacs et al. (2022; meta-analysis). The 40.3% figure comes from 2011 records at two Korean emergency departments, not a national sample. Both findings describe associations, not causation.

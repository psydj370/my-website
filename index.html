Dojin Lee

Clinician & Researcher · PhD Applicant
Suicide Prevention · Public Mental Health · Addiction

I am a clinician working in a community mental health center in South Korea. I was recently selected as a Fulbright Graduate Student Program Alternate for the 2027–2028 academic year. I earned my MA and BA in Psychology from Chungnam National University, with a minor in Computer Science during my undergraduate studies, where my research examined how psychological, genetic, and environmental factors interact over time to shape behavior. My research experience has included longitudinal research, intervention studies, meta-analysis, and intensive daily data.

My long-term goal is to help reduce suicide deaths by developing systems that can detect when an individual's risk is rising and respond in time. I am especially interested in using machine learning with intensive longitudinal data, including ecological momentary assessment (EMA) and other digital signals, to predict near-term suicide risk and deliver timely, personalized interventions.

Long-Term Direction

From prediction to prevention systems

Individual level

Understand risk

Build better models of when suicide risk rises within a specific person using repeated and real-world data rather than one-time snapshots.

Intervention level

Personalize support

Use those insights to guide support that better matches the person's needs, context, and available resources.

System level

Strengthen prevention systems

Translate research into tools and strategies that can work in hospitals, community organizations, and public mental health systems in Korea and beyond.

My long-term goal is not simply to build a better risk model. It is to help create suicide-prevention systems that are more precise, more responsive, and more capable of reaching people before a moment of crisis becomes irreversible.

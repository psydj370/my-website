Dojin Lee

Suicide Prevention · Public Mental Health · Addiction

I am a clinician working in a community mental health center in South Korea. I was recently selected as a Fulbright Graduate Student Program Alternate for the 2027–2028 academic year. I earned my MA and BA in Psychology from Chungnam National University and minored in Computer Science as an undergraduate. My research has examined how psychological, genetic, and environmental factors interact over time to shape behavior, using longitudinal studies, intervention research, meta-analysis, and intensive daily data.

My long-term goal is to help reduce suicide rates. I have spent years thinking about how to contribute to this goal, shaped in part by my experience living in South Korea, where the suicide rate remains high.

I believe meaningful prevention depends on two things: recognizing when someone is at risk before a crisis escalates and identifying how suicide-prevention hotlines and other crisis-response services can be designed and implemented to deliver timely, cost-effective support at scale.

Long-term vision

I believe reducing suicide rates requires a connected approach: detecting risk earlier, intervening in time, and linking people to community care.

My long-term vision is to help integrate these steps into real-world mental health systems through collaboration with researchers, clinicians, and community partners.
